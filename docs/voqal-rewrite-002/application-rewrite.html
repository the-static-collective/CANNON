Still Here — The First Circle Foundry

Overview 746 / 750

Still Here — The First Circle Foundry helps people without trusted networks find a safe human connection without surrendering their stories to an institution or platform. Beginning with isolated adults, including survivors of relationship abuse and family separation, the project will recruit paid lived-experience and qualified safeguarding reviewers to co-design testimony-optional, low-tech entry points. People and local stewards retain the right to refuse, withdraw and adapt the process. We have already built reproducible open-source software that tests these boundaries in fictional cases. The fellowship would fund the missing human work: independent review, actual permission, careful local listening and one bounded pilot only if safe.

Impact 1492 / 10000

A person should not need an existing network to gain access to a safe first relationship. Current systems often demand a complete story, digital account or gatekeeper's approval before a human can even ask for practical help. I want to test a different starting rule: a person may ask for one bounded next step, say no, remain silent, or withdraw; neither software nor an organizer can manufacture another person's permission. This is about changing who owns the doorway, not building a replacement intake platform.

We have developed a working experimental architecture for this principle. An October 2026 CANNON demonstration executes original code from 22 independently maintained public repositories, checks documentary sources from 64 repositories and links 69 distinct public repositories overall. It produces five fictional, reproducible scenarios; the Voqal First Circle scenario includes permission holds and distinct receiver decisions. Those are software findings, not 22 partner organizations, 69 participating people, or a live survivor program.

The structural question is whether a practice can be inherited by a new neighborhood without inheriting its founder's authority. The human question is whether that transfer is safe, useful and accountable to people with firsthand experience of isolation and coercion. Compensation and independent safeguarding review come before outreach or real-world testing. A justified decision not to launch would count as responsible learning.

Summary 1631 / 7500

APRIL — Recruit and compensate independent lived-experience reviewers and qualified safeguarding/accessibility advisers. Map local existing services, conflicts of interest, privacy risks and firm stop rules; invite participation without assuming it.

MAY — Co-design one testimony-optional, person-controlled first-contact path on paper, by phone or through an optional offline tool. Test refusal, permission and withdrawal in fictional scenarios before any intake or introduction.

JUNE — With a willing host, qualified safety review, documented permissions and informed consent, attempt one narrowly bounded, facilitated resource-navigation or introduction step. If those conditions are absent, remain in a simulated HOLD and publish only safe process learnings.

JULY — Commission independent safety, privacy and accessibility review; compare outcomes and refused or abandoned paths without treating silence as success.

AUGUST — Revise the first-circle invitation kit into an optional, low-tech, locally owned method with alternative non-digital routes and transferable review instructions.

SEPTEMBER — Invite critical evaluation of the method and evidence, publish consented findings and limitations, and make a go/no-go decision for any continuation.

The technical starting point already exists: CANNON's source-pinned 22-donor, five-scenario software machine, which can be independently replayed. It is not evidence of existing survivor clients or qualified independent governance. Grant resources would support people, safety, access, convening, learning and a carefully limited pilot rather than another platform launch.

Resources 1172 / 5000

The most important resources are people and time: paid reviewers with relevant lived experience, qualified safeguarding and facilitation expertise, local stewards willing to challenge the proposal, accessible paper/phone options, and coaching that can help me hear when a design is unsafe. The $30,000 plan allocates $12,000 for focused applicant time, $6,000 for compensated survivor/community reviewers, $3,500 for safeguarding consultation, $2,500 for accessibility, and the remaining $6,000 for print/offline access, local travel, legal/privacy review and minimal equipment. These are proposed uses, not existing commitments.

The engineering part is unusually far along for an idea-stage experiment: CANNON demonstrates a 22-owner executable integration with independently replayed fictional outcomes, and JAR;VIS now has a separate pinned operator doorway. What that architecture cannot supply is an actual safe first connection or trusted independent reviewer. Coaching and Voqal's peer cohort would be valuable precisely because they can challenge the assumptions embedded in the tools and help me work with existing communities rather than impose one more system.
