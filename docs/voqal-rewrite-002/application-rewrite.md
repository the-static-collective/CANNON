### OVERVIEW — 746/750 characters

Still Here — The First Circle Foundry helps people without trusted networks find a safe human connection without surrendering their stories to an institution or platform. Beginning with isolated adults, including survivors of relationship abuse and family separation, the project will recruit paid lived-experience and qualified safeguarding reviewers to co-design testimony-optional, low-tech entry points. People and local stewards retain the right to refuse, withdraw and adapt the process. We have already built reproducible open-source software that tests these boundaries in fictional cases. The fellowship would fund the missing human work: independent review, actual permission, careful local listening and one bounded pilot only if safe.


### IMPACT — 1493/10000 characters

A person should not need an existing network to gain access to a safe first relationship. Current systems often demand a complete story, digital account or gatekeeper's approval before a human can even ask for practical help. I want to test a different starting rule: a person may ask for one bounded next step, say no, remain silent, or withdraw; neither software nor an organizer can manufacture another person's permission. This is about changing who owns the doorway, not building a replacement intake platform.

We have developed a working experimental architecture for this principle. An October 2026 CANNON demonstration executes original code from 22 independently maintained public repositories, verifies 64 additional documentary-source inputs and links 69 distinct public repositories overall. It produces five fictional, reproducible scenarios; the Voqal First Circle scenario includes permission holds and distinct receiver decisions. Those are software findings, not 22 partner organizations, 69 participating people, or a live survivor program.

The structural question is whether a practice can be inherited by a new neighborhood without inheriting its founder's authority. The human question is whether that transfer is safe, useful and accountable to people with firsthand experience of isolation and coercion. Compensation and independent safeguarding review come before outreach or real-world testing. A justified decision not to launch would count as responsible learning.
